### What Investors Want to See in a First Meeting

- Deep founder understanding of the path from demo to deployment, not just the demo itself
  - Example: Seattle robotics co that trained a robot from a single demonstration, with an on-device RL loop improving over time
- Founder resilience and track record: tell the story of what changed from year one to year two
- Flexibility to pivot into adjacent application areas; founders who frown at new verticals are a red flag
- Humility alongside the pitch: “I know how to slice the bread after I failed 10 times”
  - Pure sales mode is tolerated for the first minute; no learnings by meeting two is a DQ
- For Honda specifically: a pre-researched collaboration idea tied to an existing Honda business unit is a standout, and a requirement for a term sheet

### Red Flags and Yellow Flags

- Conversation keeps circling back to fundraising instead of technology differentiation
- Defensibility answered with POC mentions rather than underlying tech moat
- “ChatGPT moment for robotics” framing: yellow flag; the field is still divergent, not consolidating
- All-in-one software platform claims: cheap to build, and misses the real challenge of learning with minimal data and improving on-task
- No clear line of sight to customer needs: investors are always thinking one step ahead (task → fleet → fleet improvement)

### Investor Theses and Hot Verticals

- Two priority bets right now:
  - Robot intelligence: finding the “transformer equivalent” for physical AI; world models and VLA architectures not yet proven
  - Vertical deployment: less hyped but where real-world adoption and data flywheels live
- Humanoid hype is real but no commercial deal above ~200 units deployed yet; gap between demo and useful deployment remains huge
- Excited verticals: industrial automation, elder care and healthcare (physical therapists as an evergreen unfilled job), compute and power efficiency
- Compute architecture: edge inference for latency-sensitive tasks; cloud/on-prem for pre-training and complex reasoning
  - Dark factories: impressive but niche; real robotics opportunity is unstructured/semi-structured tasks previously too hard for machines

### Founder Advice for Raising in the Next Six Months

- Expect a long journey: robotics startups are orders of magnitude harder than pure software
- Start talking to strategics early, even at pre-seed or stealth, to get technical validation and a deployment reference
- Know your customer’s total cost of ownership: compare current human labor cost vs. robot cost at scale; marginal cost improves with repetition
- Unit economics are the deciding factor for large-scale deployment; deep workflow integration is the defensible moat (analogous to SaaS 10–15 years ago)
- Always pitch one step ahead of where you are: if the robot completes a task, the investor is already asking if the fleet can improve on the field
