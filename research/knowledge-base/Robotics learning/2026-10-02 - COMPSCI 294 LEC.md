### Red Team: Offline RL and AWAC

- Core problem: bootstrapping error accumulation in offline RL
  - Critic evaluates out-of-distribution actions, propagating errors to actor
  - Naive fix: constrain policy close to behavior policy via KL divergence
  - But over-constraining slows policy improvement when online data arrives
- AWAC as middle ground
  - Advantage-weighted policy update: weights actions from replay buffer by estimated advantage
  - Lagrangian formulation yields closed-form optimal policy
  - KL projection into neural network parameter space
  - Key insight: expectation under behavior distribution sampled directly from replay buffer, no explicit behavior model needed

### Blue Team: Is Imitation Learning Enough?

- IL vs. RL comparison across four dimensions:
  - Learning signal: IL uses expert actions directly; RL reward is often sparse (0/1 at episode end)
  - Exploration: IL needs none (all states from human data); RL discovers actions from scratch
  - Credit assignment: IL labels every action; RL faces long-horizon drift
  - Ceiling: IL bounded by expert; RL can exceed expert via reward signal
- Blue team’s stance: IL with human-in-the-loop is enough for most real robot skills when expert data is available
  - First fix for a failing policy: improve data coverage, not switch to RL
  - RL warranted when no expert exists or expert data can’t complete the task

### Behavior Cloning: Diffusion Policy Example

- Standard BC: collect human demos, train policy offline on fixed dataset
- PI-zero extension: uses diffusion to denoise action chunks guided by camera
  - Adds success filter (not a reward signal) to generate robot-produced data
  - Mixes robot successes with human data to retrain PI-1
- Result: policy retries on failure but rarely self-recovers without human intervention
  - Human-in-the-loop drives meaningful improvement

### Interactive Imitation: HG-Dagger

- Task: car weaving through parked vehicles on a two-lane road
- Pure BC result: frequent off-road failures
- HG-Dagger fix: human acts as gating function, intervening when policy enters dangerous states
  - Labels out-of-distribution states with expert corrections
  - Retrains policy on targeted failure data
  - Result: collision rate and road-deviation rate drop near zero
- Dagger vs. HG-Dagger discussion
  - Dagger is theoretically convergent to optimal policy but underperforms HG-Dagger in practice
  - Likely violated assumption: algorithm-controlled switching between human and policy is noisier than human-controlled gating
  - HG-Dagger lets human decide when to intervene, avoiding over-compensation artifacts
- Secondary result: quantified policy uncertainty as a risk map (red = unsafe zones near road edges and obstacles)

### Spoiler Team: Agentic Robotics with LLMs

- Four roles for LLMs in robotics:
  1. Task planning: translate high-level goals into structured subtasks for low-level controllers
  2. Code generation: write executable policies directly (Codex Policies, 2022)
  3. Agentic improvement: reflect on failures, call tools (IK, SAM), iterate on reward functions
  4. Direct low-level control: output 6-DoF end-effector poses at ~6-10 Hz, passed to IK controller
- Agentic loop (this-year paper): agent selects IL or RL autonomously based on what works
  - Humans provide environment, verification, and resets; agent handles the rest
- Direct VLM control result: top performance on robot manipulation benchmark, especially on generalization
  - Still lags on precision and coordinated bimanual control
- Key claim: if VLM already encodes action knowledge, it is functionally equivalent to a VLA

### Discussion: RL vs. IL Trade-offs

- Effort graph debate: IL requires more human data collection; RL requires reward/environment engineering
  - Both plateau at similar total effort in the middle range
  - But effort graph misses the policy quality dimension: some domains require 99.999% success
- Optimal vs. epsilon-optimal framing
  - Industry view: ship when good enough (epsilon); scale compute across capabilities
  - Research view: prove convergence, reach true optimal
  - Counterpoint: even large labs (Google, Anthropic) face resource trade-offs across capabilities
- RL as the only path in some cases
  - Robot joints can move 10x faster than humans: no expert teleop data possible
  - Physics of acrobatic motions (humanoid flips) cannot be captured by IL alone; RL needed for dynamics
- RLHF / RL-VR framing: still RL, just with a human-learned reward model
  - Imitation learning reframed as RL where reward = closeness to demonstration (mathematically distinct but conceptually adjacent)
- Long-horizon tasks and the 99% to 100% gap flagged as key open problems for RL
