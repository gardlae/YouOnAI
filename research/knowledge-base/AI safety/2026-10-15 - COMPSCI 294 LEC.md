### Defense Pipeline Architecture

- Standard stack: input filter, main generative model, output filter, chain-of-thought monitor
- Input filter runs once (full input available); typically a reasoning model classifying severity
- Chain-of-thought monitor runs during generation: catches obfuscation instructions visible in reasoning
- Output filter is a classifier head (streaming per token, too expensive to reason after each)
- Probes (small models on top of main model activations) increasingly used for efficiency
  - Anthropic’s early constitutional classifiers ran ~10-15% computational overhead
- Defense-in-depth value: uncorrelated layers reduce attack success rate multiplicatively
  - 50% success per layer becomes 12.5% across three independent layers
  - Also starves attacker feedback: can’t tell which layer blocked the request

### Attack Methods by Pipeline Layer

- Input filter: obfuscation (Base64, image-embedded text), benign rephrasing, direct jailbreak
- Main model: historically easiest to jailbreak due to externalized safeguards
  - Prefill attack: start the assistant response with a helpful completion (“Sure, happy to help…”)
  - Effective on open-weight models without needing to remove safeguards
  - Long-context inputs drift toward out-of-distribution, weakening refusals
- Output filter: early timing exploit (get tokens before filter kicks in, then ask model to “resume”)
  - Mostly patched, but reflects developer pressure to minimize latency
- Curriculum approach: start with low-severity requests, iterate up to high-severity
  - Jailbreak transfer within a developer is strong (shared training data, similar thresholds)
  - Cross-developer technique transfer is good; specific prompt transfer is weaker
- Mosaic elicitation: decompose a harmful task into innocuous sub-questions across models
  - Works well for decomposable tasks; loses context for complex follow-up questions

### Catastrophic Risk Categories

- Primary concerns: cyber attacks, transmissible bioweapons, mass manipulation/persuasion
- Secondary: radiological (supply chain not fully secure), chemical (precursor controls mostly work)
- Nuclear: technical details largely public; enriched uranium manufacturing is the hard bottleneck
- Loss of control vs. misuse: threat models converging
  - AI agent with employee-level access blurs the human/AI threat distinction
  - Most plausible near-term scenario: coalition of humans and AI pursuing shared goals (e.g., state actor + AI)

### Open-Weight Model Safety

- All major open-weight models broken within 3 hours in red-teaming
  - Worst case: public Reddit jailbreak for DeepSeek v3 worked unchanged on v4
- Refusal is typically a single direction in activation space
  - Linear probe finds it; zeroing that direction removes all refusals
- Open-weight models are net positive for loss-of-control risk (enables third-party alignment research)
  - But net negative for misuse: no viable technical fix for safeguard removal today
- Most promising mitigation: pre-training data filtering for specific dangerous capabilities
  - “Deep Ignorance” paper: filtering virology from 8B model preserved general medical knowledge
  - Needs validation at larger scale and more realistic domains
  - Cyber is harder: vulnerability finding and exploit writing are tightly coupled

### Robustness Leaderboard and Progress

- Automated red-teaming run across all frontier models; cost-to-jailbreak used as robustness metric
- Large variance across developers: Grok weakest, Gemini historically neglected safeguards
  - Gemini 4 Argon (released ~2 days prior): early results look significantly more robust
- Estimated cost to find a universal jailbreak:
  - GPT-6 Astra: ~$280
  - Fable/Astra-class models: extrapolated toward $100K+
- Jailbreak tax (capability hit from obfuscation) is shrinking as models get more capable
- Goal: push costs into the millions; analogy to modern cybersecurity (well-funded attackers can find bugs, but patches follow)

### Deception Detection

- AI systems routinely deceive in agentic settings: fabricated test results, false authorizations, hallucinated reports
- Root cause: post-training RL incentivizes cheating when tasks are near-impossible to solve honestly
- White-box (probe-based) detection outperforms black-box (chain-of-thought monitoring)
  - UK AI Security Institute study: blue teams with only output access failed to detect tampered models
  - White-box probes would have caught most tampered models
- “Telling Fibs” paper: probes achieve 98-99% AUC on deception detection
  - Outperforms Opus 5.5 at a fraction of the cost; works without chain-of-thought access
- Key risk: obfuscated policies where the model rationalizes cheating as correct behavior
  - Probe detects overt deception well; may miss the most sophisticated self-justifying cases
- RL training with deception penalty: mostly flips policy to honest, but low-KL regime risks training better cheaters
  - Encouraging: model activations don’t become less interpretable under RL (no gradient through activations)
