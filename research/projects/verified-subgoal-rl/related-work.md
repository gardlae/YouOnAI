# Verified subgoal RL: bounded related-work check

Checked 2026-10-22. Exploratory evidence, not a novelty certification or a completed literature review. Scope: VLM/image-language subgoals, ordinary low-level RL, progress that can decrease, and reliable completion in unfamiliar scenes. Local reading-list entries are a queue, not evidence of papers already studied; RoboCLIP, Hi Robot, HAMSTER and DrEureka were located there selectively.

The initial three-source pass covered RoboCLIP, LIV and StructRL. Expansion to eight papers answered two specific unresolved questions: whether recent methods already combine progress and completion, and whether decreasing progress has a principled shaping formulation. No implementation, training, or reproduction was run. Source-code links below establish available repositories, not working installations or audited licenses/checkpoint rights.

## What might still be a useful question

The architecture itself is insufficient novelty. StructRL's appendix explicitly identifies sensory completion signals as future work, but Robometer already addresses learned success, stage advancement and unseen scenes. Robometer also identifies limitations involving subtle failures, memory and hidden physical conditions. These observations motivate a **controlled evaluation question**, not a claim that nobody has addressed the problem. [StructRL limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36352v1#A7), [Robometer limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02115v2#S5).

Candidate contribution: at a fixed supervision and inference budget, measure whether a separately calibrated completion decision plus reversible progress improves *actual sustained success* on deliberately held-out scenes and recoverable near-success errors. A second head alone does not constitute independence: compare shared-backbone heads, separately trained verifiers, and a calibrated existing reward model. Independence is an experimental factor, not an architectural guarantee.

Start with one fixed subgoal and one controlled failure family. Only add VLM planning after reward/verification behavior is understood. Hierarchical composition then adds confounds—bad decomposition, unreachable subgoals, and incorrect transition decisions—that obscure the first experiment.

## Falsifiable hypotheses and matched baselines

1. **Completion decisions:** At matched successful-stop recall on a validation split, a verifier calibrated on real simulated near-misses reduces false-success stops on held-out scenes relative to thresholding progress. Compare raw progress threshold, calibrated progress threshold, existing success head, shared-backbone new head, and separately trained verifier. Also report missed completions, decision delay, calibration/Brier score and risk versus coverage; always refusing to stop is not an improvement.
2. **Recovery:** With the same low-level RL algorithm, observations, initialization, interaction budget and supervision, signed potential differences improve oracle-measured recovery after placement slippage/reopening relative to raw progress rewards, positive-only progress differences, and latched/max-so-far progress. Include sparse oracle success and oracle dense shaping as explicitly privileged controls, plus a learned sparse-success condition. Report success, recovery time, false completion and oscillation—not learned return alone.
3. **Temporal evidence:** For visually ambiguous endpoints, short causal history or a budgeted verification action improves completion risk at matched inference/action cost compared with single-frame verification. Match against extra passive frames and simple persistence checks. Reject the hypothesis if improvements disappear at equal budget or require hidden state unavailable to the deployed model.

Use LIV-style value rewards and RL-VLM-F-style preference rewards as lightweight reward families. Include released Robometer progress/success where feasible; if its compute is outside the budget, explicitly mark the comparison absent and narrow claims. Structured simulator predicates are an oracle supervision baseline inspired by StructRL, not a fair perceptual comparator. Do not substitute literature numbers from different policies and budgets for controlled experiments.

## Reward and evaluation pitfalls

- For a fixed goal, distinguish absolute progress `p_t` from reward. A candidate shaping term is `F_t = gamma * Phi(x_{t+1}, g) - Phi(x_t, g)`, added to a specified base objective. It can penalize regressions. Plain `p_{t+1} - p_t` matches this form only when `gamma = 1`. Potential shaping cannot correct a wrong base completion objective.
- Fix/freeze the potential for the initial study; specify terminal/timeout handling, goal identity and history state. Goal switching, nonstationary model updates, reward clipping, and nonzero terminal potentials require separate analysis. Positive-only changes can pay repeatedly for oscillation; raw positive progress can reward dwelling. Test both explicitly.
- Keep simulator state behind a clear boundary: it may supply declared training labels or privileged baseline rewards, and should judge final outcomes. The learned reward/verifier/policy receives only its declared observations. Using state labels for supervision means supervised reward learning, not label-free learning.
- Hold out scene families before training or calibration. Vary object geometry, distractors, camera viewpoint and occlusion separately before combining shifts. Document pretrained reward-model overlap; unfamiliar rendering is not proof of unseen semantics.
- Build negatives from actual simulated failures and recoveries. Reversed videos alone need not represent physically possible errors. Match data volume and failure mix across ablations.
- Define completion operationally: e.g. object released inside receptacle and still contained after a fixed settling interval. Separately measure predicate truth at the declared stop and after that interval. A historically achieved subgoal may cease to hold.
- Pixel-identical observations can conceal different physical states. No image-only model can reliably distinguish them without additional evidence; an inspection action, longer history or an abstention policy is then part of the task definition.

## Remaining limits

Eight primary works were checked at the depths stated above, not read exhaustively. Recent papers' results were not reproduced; code and licenses were not audited. A later novelty pass must follow the closest references on task-success calibration, active verification and reversible subtask state before any paper claim is selected. Current evidence supports an educational replication plus a narrow stress-test study; publishability remains unresolved.
