SILICON GOLD — First comic plan

Premise
In the year 2200, a man arrives in Silicon Valley searching for gold.
Humanity has lost much of its technological knowledge. His excavation
uncovers an old data center filled with NVIDIA GPUs. He cannot understand
why anyone built these enormous structures in what is now wilderness.

Format
One short comic page: six panels, read left to right, top to bottom.
Working interpretation of "different cartoons": separate scenes in a
continuous story, using a consistent character and illustration style.
The title, dialogue, and ending below are proposed creative choices.

Visual direction
Hand-drawn adventure cartoon with expressive ink lines and muted earth
colors. Green circuit boards provide a striking visual reveal.
The miner wears a patched, rough-woven tunic, a rope belt, worn foot wraps,
and a small cloth pack. He carries a simple iron pick with a wooden handle.
Keep his face, clothing, tools, and proportions consistent across panels.
The landscape is dry grass, hills, and partially buried concrete ruins.
No functioning futuristic city: the date and the ruins establish the future.
Do not explain the cause of humanity's decline; leave it mysterious.

Storyboard

1. ARRIVAL — wide establishing shot
The small figure crosses an empty valley toward half-buried ruins.
A weathered sign reads "SILICON VALLEY."
Caption: "Silicon Valley. 2200."
Miner: "They said this valley made men rich."

2. THE DIG — medium action shot
He digs beside a concrete edge protruding from the hillside.
Miner: "There must be gold down here."
Sound: "CLINK"

3. THE FIRST FIND — close-up
He brushes earth from a corroded circuit board. Its gold-colored connector
contacts catch the light. Reveal only part of the hardware at first.
Miner: "Gold...?"

4. THE REVEAL — largest, most visually detailed panel
His excavation opens into a buried server room. Rows of tilted racks,
heatsinks, cables, and GPU boards extend into the darkness.
One foreground board bears a legible "NVIDIA" label for the reader.
The hardware is abandoned, damaged, and unpowered.
No dialogue: let the reader recognize what he has found.

5. THE QUESTION — medium shot, looking through the racks
Holding the board, he looks up at the incomprehensible structure.
Miner: "Who built all this... out in the middle of nowhere?"

6. THE ENDING — quiet close-up
He studies the tiny gold-colored contacts against the vast ruined machinery.
Miner: "All this trouble for so little gold."
The irony: the reader understands that the machines were once the treasure.

Production steps
1. Establish the six-panel script and visual direction with this plan.
2. Generate one complete rough comic page using the built-in image tool.
   A single page helps maintain character and style continuity.
3. Inspect panel order, character consistency, the GPU reveal, and lettering.
4. Revise unclear panels using the first page as a visual reference.
   Keep speech short and large enough to read on screen.
5. Save the chosen page inside this Comic folder and display it inline.
6. If separate panel images are desired, generate each from the approved
   visual reference and retain the same character and environment details.
