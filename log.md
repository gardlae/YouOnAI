# AI-use disclosure: regulating open AI models

**Prepared:** 2026-10-19, 10:23 PDT (America/Los_Angeles)  
**AI tool/model:** OpenAI Codex, GPT-6 family (the exact variant is not exposed in this chat)  
**Research question:** How should the US and EU balance safety rules for open AI models against consumer choice and possible lower prices?

## Short tutor-style conversation

**Human (actual request):** Research regulation of open-source AI models, focusing on safety versus more diverse consumer choice and cheaper prices. Use a Socratic tutor style, identify a hidden assumption, ask a clarifying question, and do not supply a final policy answer.

**AI (actual clarification):** Which jurisdiction should anchor the research: the US, the EU, or a comparison of both?

**Human (actual decision):** Compare US and EU.

**AI (research response, 2026-10-19):** The EU AI Act gives qualifying free and open-source general-purpose models a limited documentation exemption, but still requires a copyright policy and a training-content summary; models with systemic risk do not get that exemption. In the US, California's SB 53 requires certain frontier developers to publish safety frameworks and report critical incidents. A 2024 US Commerce Department report says widely available model weights can lower entry costs for smaller developers and support competition, while also creating possible safety risks; it does not establish that consumer prices will fall. These are different policy approaches and possible effects, not a verdict on which approach is best. [EU Commission guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/guidelines-obligations-general-purpose-ai-providers), [California governor's SB 53 summary](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/09/29/governor-newsom-signs-sb-53-advancing-californias-world-leading-artificial-intelligence-industry/), [US NTIA competition analysis](https://www.ntia.gov/programs-and-initiatives/artificial-intelligence/open-model-weights-report/risks-benefits-of-dual-use-foundation-models-with-widely-available-model-weights/competition-innovation-research).

**AI (Socratic follow-up):** One hidden assumption is that more available models will automatically mean lower prices for consumers. What evidence would you need to show that lower developer costs actually reach consumers?

**Human (next step):** Answer that question and choose the policy position yourself; no response or final position has been supplied yet.
